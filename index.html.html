Understanding Ransomware Threats

Ransomware is a type of malicious software (malware) designed to lock your personal files or computer system until you pay a ransom to cybercriminals.

How Ransomware Attacks Work Step-by-Step

1

Initial Infection

The user accidentally triggers the malware by clicking a fake link in a phishing email, opening a malicious email attachment, or downloading unverified software.

2

Silent Encryption

The malware silently runs in the background, encrypting (scrambling) photos, documents, spreadsheets, and system files into unreadable code.

3

Ransom Demand

A ransom note appears on screen demanding payment (usually via cryptocurrency) within a strict deadline to receive a decryption key.

Primary Infection Vectors

  • Phishing Emails (80%+ of cases) Emails pretending to be trusted sources containing malicious links or attached ZIP/PDF/EXE files.
  • Drive-by Downloads & Malicious Ads Visiting compromised websites or clicking fake online advertisements that secretly download malware.
  • Unsecured Remote Access (RDP) Hackers scan for weak passwords on remote desktop services to directly gain access to systems.
Should You Pay the Ransom?

Security experts and law enforcement agencies (such as CISA and the FBI) strongly advise NEVER paying the ransom.

There is no guarantee criminals will send the decryption key.
Paying funds criminal organizations and fuels future cybercrime.
Victims who pay are frequently targeted again.
"Prevention and proper offline backups are your only reliable defense."

Dangerous File Extension Flags

Double Extensions

Invoice.pdf.exe

Hides executable programs behind familiar file names like PDF or Word.

Script Files

.vbs / .js / .bat

Scripting files that execute malware commands immediately upon opening.

Macro Enabled

.docm / .xlsm

Office documents configured to trigger malicious background scripts.

Encrypted Files

.locked / .crypto

New extension appended to your files indicating ransomware has encrypted them.

4 Pillars of Ransomware Prevention

By implementing these four core cybersecurity practices, you dramatically reduce the risk of a successful ransomware infection.

1. Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)

Enforce MFA on all accounts. Even if an attacker steals your login password via a phishing link, they cannot access your account without the secondary authentication factor (e.g., authenticator app code).

Blocks 99% of automated account takeover attempts.

2. Regular Software Updates & Patching

Keep operating systems, web browsers, and applications updated to the latest versions. Enable automatic updates whenever possible to fix known security vulnerabilities before hackers exploit them.

Closes known system doors used by drive-by malware.

3. The 3-2-1 Backup Strategy

Maintain 3 copies of important data on 2 different media types, with 1 copy stored offsite/offline (disconnected from the network). Backups neutralized ransomware threats completely.

Ensures full data recovery without paying any ransom.

4. Vigilant Email & Web Browsing

Never click links or open attachments from unexpected or unverified senders. When in doubt, verify the communication directly through an official channel (e.g., call the sender directly using a known number).

Your personal awareness is the most critical firewall.
